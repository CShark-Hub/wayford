## Notes per step

**1. RULES.**
- The B4 row in stage 7: Roll `misclick`. Effect: "for a click on a control smaller than the persona's motor limit, with a control in reach, with probability `p` from the effective-width model the click lands on the nearest such control". Source: "ISO 9241-9 effective width, MacKenzie 1992; error model, Wobbrock et al. 2008; the persona's limit carries its own provenance; the time penalty is s2-09's (Fitts's law)".
- The firing-rule bullet already says "the effective-width model for B4"; keep it.
- A paragraph **B4 in detail** under the table. It holds:
  - `W`, `L`, `σ = L / 4.133`, `k`, the formula `p = erf(k) − erf(k · W / L)`;
  - the worked examples, as the spec gives them, computed with Python's `math.erf`;
  - the assumptions: the reading of the limit as an effective width is `folk`; the model is mouse and 1-D; distance and speed are ignored; baseline misses are not modelled;
  - the aim point, the candidates, the distance, the reach, nearest and ties, and where the press lands;
  - what B4 gets wrong;
  - B2 after a miss;
  - the order B1, B2, B4;
  - the series for `erf`, with its recurrence and the literal.
- A paragraph **P4 and B4**: the engineering division; "P4 floor"; no persona sets a limit yet; the next step and what it needs first.
- Replace "B4 is the only behaviour rule with real science behind it. B1 and B2 are folk and say so." as the spec's "RULES changes" says.
- RULES' voice: plain sentences, the source in each claim, no claim of prediction.

**3. `misclick.ts`.**
- `MISS_SCALE = 4.133 / (2 * Math.SQRT2)`.
- `missProbability(W, L) = erf(MISS_SCALE) − erf(MISS_SCALE * W / L)`, for `0 ≤ W < L`. It throws a `RangeError` outside that, since only a bug passes one.
- `erf(x)`: the series exactly as the spec writes it. Private: not exported, and tested through `missProbability`.
- `misclickOf(ref, page, limitPx)`:
  - Find the element with that ref, `state` not invisible.
  - Return undefined when there is none, when it has no `metrics.targetPx`, or when `targetPx >= limitPx`.
  - The aim point is `actionPoint(target.bbox, page.viewport)` from `act.ts`.
  - Candidates are the elements of `page.elements` with a ref, `state` not invisible, and a defined `metrics.targetPx`. Drop the target itself, a candidate whose box holds the aim point, and a candidate whose `actionPoint` lies inside the target's box (boxes are half-open, `[x, x + width)`, as the viewport is).
  - The distance is Euclidean, from the aim point to the nearest point of the candidate's box.
  - Keep a candidate with distance `<= limitPx`. Take the smallest distance, and the first in `page.elements` order on a tie.
  - Return `{ p: missProbability(targetPx, limitPx), neighbour: candidate.ref }`, or undefined when none is kept.
- Tests, on small hand-built `FullObservation`s. Build them with a helper in the test file and parse them with core's `FullObservation`, so they are real evidence. Cover:
  - `p` at (22, 44), (24, 48), (30, 44), (36, 44), (46, 48), (0, 48) to 6 places, values from the spec;
  - `p` is 0 at `W = L`;
  - the nearest control wins over a farther one;
  - a text block (no `targetPx`) nearer than any control is never chosen;
  - an invisible control is never chosen;
  - an enclosing control is skipped;
  - a control whose action point is inside the target's box is skipped;
  - a tie goes to the earlier element;
  - a control just at `L` counts, and one just beyond does not;
  - no control in reach gives undefined;
  - a target with no `targetPx`, or at or above the limit, gives undefined;
  - an unknown ref gives undefined.

**4. `behave` and the loop.**
