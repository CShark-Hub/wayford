# s4-08 — Rehearsal on our site: every screen, from the Target screen: spec

Task: `tasks/s4-08-public-site-run.md` · Branch: `feature/s4-08-rehearsal` · Date: 2026-10-09
Status: approved 2026-10-09

## Goal

Take our pick, cshark.com, through every story screen from the Target screen, the way the finals will, on its two read-only goals. Record the results and every gap the UI hits that `s1-07` did not, each with its issue, blocking or not.

## Constraints that bind

- "A read-only goal: no form that saves, no login, no purchase, nothing that writes to the site" (task, Constraints; ADR 0003, "Not in").
- "Every run on the LLM: the race, the run set, Scout and the sweep" (task; ADR 0003 D9). io refuses Jev off the target app anyway.
- "Started from the Target screen, as the finals will be. A step that needs the CLI, other than the sweep, is itself a gap" (task).
- Code changes only "for a fix under S size that unblocks a screen. Bigger fixes go to `s1-07`" (task, Interface). A gap larger than S is listed and goes back to `s1-07`'s list, or is cut (Out of scope). No route table for the site (Out of scope).
- A sweep varies the persona, never the site (`docs/PLAN.md` F9 AC4).
- "Claim less than you'd like to." `docs/DEMO.md` says what each success condition proves and what it does not, as `s1-07` did.
- `.env` holds the key. The user places it in the worktree root; the agent never reads, copies or tests for it.
- Merge cutoff: Tuesday 2026-10-13. None of this may delay a demo task (ADR 0003, Consequences).

## Design

### 1. The site and its goals

The site is `s1-07`'s pick: `https://www.cshark.com/`, the start URL of both goals. Checked again on 2026-10-09: the job offer page and `/about/` answer 200, and the listing `/careers/job-offers/` still shows the role with "125 - 145 PLN/h + VAT B2B". Each pass checks it again before it starts (`s1-07` spec, section 2, step 0).

Two goals, the task's two. They are `s1-07`'s G1 and G2, typed in again on the Target screen. The stories are kept word for word.

| # | Story, typed in the goal field | Success, URL pattern | What success proves, and what it does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 | "Find the names of the company's board members." | `https://www.cshark.com/about/**` | As in `s1-07`: the run reached `/about/`, which names the board in its "Founders" section about 2,300 px down. It does not prove the persona read the names. |
| G2 | "Find out what the salary is for the Senior Performance Test Engineer role." | `https://www.cshark.com/{careers/job-offers/**,job-offers/senior-performance-test-engineer/**}` | The run reached the listing, whose card for the role states the rate, or the offer itself, which states it near its top. G2 is widened to the listing, as `s1-07` recommended: in two of its runs Ada read the rate off the listing's card and gave up there, and the record said `gave_up` where the answer had been read (`docs/DEMO.md`, second pass, G2). ### 2. The rehearsal, per goal

All of it runs under `pnpm dev` in this worktree, started as `env -u PORT pnpm dev` in the background, so io keeps port 3000, web 5173 and the target app 5174, and stopped by PID afterwards. The database (`wayford.sqlite`) and `runs/` are the worktree's own, both ignored by git, so the rehearsal starts with nothing stored, as on a fresh machine.

Before io starts, the user confirms that their `.env` leaves `LLM_ACTOR_BACKEND` unset or `llm`, as `docs/DEMO.md` asks before the demo. The Race sends no backend, so io takes that setting, and on `jev` it refuses a race off the target app with a 422 (`packages/io/src/routes/runs.ts`). That refusal would read as a gap when it is a setting.

The browser is the Playwright MCP browser, driven like a person, by clicks and typing. Its pages count as visible, so the Target screen's polling and TanStack's retries do not pause, as they would in a hidden pane.

Each goal goes through these steps in order. Nothing else runs on the machine meanwhile, so one step's load does not slow another's runs.

The order differs from the finals' flow (`docs/DEMO.md`, "Finals: any target"), which opens the Lens and races before Scout and the set. On our pick the ten-seed report is prepared before the finals, so the path that has to be timed is the preparation: the check, Scout, the set and the report, with nothing in between. The Lens and the Race come after it, and each screen is reached the same way either order.

1. **Target, the form.** Open `/target` from the nav (`00 Target`). Type the start URL, the story and the success pattern, and press "Check the goal". The wall-time clock starts at that click. Record the goal io stored, whether it was rewritten, and what was taken out. On a refusal (422), the story is reworded with the team. The check is never weakened.
2. **Scout.** "Run Scout" in the goal view, and wait for its line. Record the line: the shortest path's steps, or the attempts made.
3. **The run set.** Cast Ada, Kai and Vera, "10 seeds", "Start 30 runs on the LLM". Keep the page open until every run has ended and the Analyst has written the report, then "Open the report". The clock stops when the report is drawn.
4. **The Report.** Record the headline per persona: got through k/10, the median simulated time, route open, which reads "—" off the target app. Record the Scout line, the axe findings (`s4-09` scans at most ten pages, those the most runs visited), the findings by type, and the cost in all that the screen states. Open one finding's evidence into run detail, play its replay, and check that the video and trace download.
5. **The Lens**, by "Open in the Lens" and then by the nav's `02 Lens`. On each page below, all five personas, seed 1, plus the evidence view for Vera and Kai. Pages are opened by the Lens's own URL field, not by editing the address bar.
   - G1: the start page, then `https://www.cshark.com/about/`.
   - G2: `https://www.cshark.com/careers/job-offers/`. The start page is not repeated.
   - Record per page and persona what the readout says reaches the model, and anything the screen draws wrong.
6. **The Race**, by the nav's `03 Race`: Ada, Kai and Vera, seed 1, on the LLM, watched to the end. Record each tile's outcome, steps and simulated time, and two lines of narration.
7. **The nav.** Between the steps above, the person moves by the top bar only. Each of `00 Target`, `02 Lens`, `03 Race` and `04 Report` must lead to this goal (ADR 0003, D13). A screen that loses the goal is a gap.

The three pages cover both goals' start, G1's goal page and G2's listing. Those are the pages the runs of `s1-07` spent their steps on.

### 3. The sweep

One sweep, on G1, prepared with the sweep CLI while io is up, so `GET /sweeps` sees it, then opened on the Boundary by its id, `/boundary/<sweepId>`. The CLI prints the id on its `sweep` line, and no screen links to a sweep by id. Typing that link into the address bar is the designed path (ADR 0003 D6, and D13 leaves `05 Boundary` unchanged), part of the task's one CLI exception, so it is not a gap under section 5's condition 4.

```
pnpm --filter @wayford/io sweep -- --task <G1's task id> --persona prof_kai --axis maxElements --from 11 --to 3 --step 2 --seeds 1-5 --backend llm
```

Kai's attention cap, from his own 11 down to 3, laxest first: five values, five seeds each, 25 runs. G1 is the goal Kai got through in one step in `s1-07` (3/3), by the header's "About". A lower cap leaves fewer elements in view, so a lower cap may close the goal. The boundary is the first value where fewer than half of Kai's runs get through: the agent's number. Off the target app there is no route table, so the screen has no route-open line beside it. Whether "About" left Kai's view at a value is said only where the stored observations of that value's runs show it. If no value falls under 50%, the screen says "no boundary down to 3", which is a result too.

**The sweep and demo beat 6.** The Boundary's `maxElements` preset, which beat 6 switches to, opened the newest stored sweep of Kai on that axis, from any task (`packages/web/src/boundary/axis.ts`, `BOUNDARY_PRESETS`; `api/sweeps.ts`). `pnpm seed:demo` refuses a store that holds any run, report or sweep, and `--reset` deletes them all, so on the presenting machine our pick's data can only come after the demo's, and the cshark sweep would take beat 6's place. That blocks the Boundary in beat 6 (#228). Its fix is under S, so it is built here (plan, "Amendment after the branch review"): each preset names the hard goal, and `/boundary` opens the newest sweep of its persona and axis on that task. `docs/DEMO.md`, "Before the finals", puts `seed:demo` first.

A contrast sweep on Vera was the other choice. It was not taken: Vera's G1 is closed by her target cut (P4) whatever the contrast, and a contrast sweep on G2 would take three times as long for a curve whose cause is not contrast.

### 4. A watch for typing

The team ran `s1-07` without a write guard, with a watch that kills io on the first typed field (`docs/specs/2026-10-07-s1-07-our-pick-gaps.md`, section 2). The same holds here, adapted to runs started from the UI.

- A throwaway script in the session scratchpad polls `GET /races` every 3 s. For each run of a race whose `status` is `running`, it follows `GET /runs/<id>/events`.
- On the first `actionPlanned` whose `performedAction.type` is `type`, on any host, it kills io's `tsx watch` process tree by PID.
- The race and the run set are both `POST /runs`, stored as races, so the watch sees both.
- Scout and the sweep CLI start no race io's runner knows of, so the watch cannot see them live. After they end, every step of every Scout attempt (`runs/ground-truth/attempts/<run id>/result.json`, failed attempts included, since the ground-truth file keeps a path only for a success) and of every stored sweep run is read for a `type` action, and `docs/DEMO.md` says what was found.

A typed site search is a read, but it stops the set as well, and the team decides about the rest. The watch reacts to typing; it does not prevent it.

### 5. Gaps and triage

A gap is a place where a screen, or a run it shows, differs from the page a person sees, or where the finals' flow cannot go on from the screen. Known gaps are #124 to #127, #146, #149, #220, #224 and #225, and those `docs/DEMO.md` lists as not fixed. A gap is new when no issue and no line there covers it.

A gap **blocks** a screen when, on this site, it does one of these:

1. It ends a run `error`, so the run says nothing about the persona.
2. It makes a success or failure claim false, by `s1-07`'s conditions 2 and 3: the model reads or reaches what a person could not, or misses what a person within the persona's thresholds would see.
3. Ada, the control, misses a goal for a reason in the engine, not the site. As in `s1-07`, a goal Ada misses on most seeds is investigated before it is put down to the site.
4. The screen cannot go on: an error state, a hang past its own timeout, a number missing that it should show, or a step that needs the CLI or the address bar.

Everything else is listed as not blocking, with one line of why.

Two leads from `s1-07` are checked on purpose:

- **A cut part no screen shows** (`docs/DEMO.md`, "Not fixed, and not blocking", "For `s4-08`"). On G2, Vera's evidence for the open menu's "Job Offers" link holds a cut 14 px part. The Lens measures a page as it loads, with the "Careers" menu closed, so only run detail can show that link: check it on the step of a Vera run where the menu is open. If it does not show the cut part, it is listed with a new issue, not blocking: it hides evidence, and changes no result.
- **A new tab.** A run that opens one is recorded, by the sign `s1-07` named (section 5): a click whose `urlAfter` equals the URL it was made on, followed by an unchanged observation, then the trace. `s2-14` is then offered to the team, and nothing for it is built here.

Each new gap gets a `[Gap]` issue, shown to the user before it is filed (manual mode).

### 6. Fixes

The gaps are not known until the rehearsal has run. The plan approved at gate 2 runs up to the triage. After it, the plan gets one step per gap to fix here, and the user approves that amendment before any fix is built.

- A fix here is under S, unblocks a screen, and comes with a test that fails before it.
- Anything bigger is listed as blocking and not fixed in `docs/DEMO.md`, with its issue, and goes back to `s1-07`'s list or is cut. The user is told the same day: a blocking gap may touch the finals' floor, the Lens, the Race and a report on our pick.
- After a fix, the screen step that hit the gap is repeated. Its result goes beside the gap.

### 7. The record in `docs/DEMO.md`

Under "Our pick: cshark.com", a new part, "Rehearsal through the UI, 2026-10-09 (`s4-08`)", holding:

- the two goals, with G2's widened pattern and why, and that G3 is not rehearsed;
- per goal: the task id, the goal io stored, the Scout line, and the run set's race id;
- per goal and persona: got through k/10, the outcomes by kind, the median simulated time; then the report's headline and its findings by type. The ten seeds of a persona are counted, not tabled cell by cell. The race id lists the runs (`GET /races/<id>`).
- per goal: the Race's three tiles and its race id;
- the Lens: a table of page by persona with what reaches the model;
- the sweep: its id, its value table, the boundary as the screen states it, and its cost;
- wall time from "Check the goal" to the drawn report, broken into the check, Scout, the set and the Analyst. The check is timed by hand; the rest comes from stored timestamps where they exist: Scout's `recordedAt`, the race's `startedAt`, the report's `analysedAt`. The cost: Scout's three attempts, summed from their `RunResult`s, and the runs' and the Analyst's, split as `GET /reports/<taskId>` stores them (the screen shows the total only). The Extractor records no cost, which the record says;
- every gap: what it is, its issue, blocking or not and which screen, and fixed or not;
- what the typing check found, and whether any run opened a new tab;
- what the numbers do not say. Run ids are local to this machine. The site may change. G1 and G2 succeed on reaching a page, not on reading it.

The finals section's line on our pick changes from "its three goals" to the two the finals use. One line there says that the stored data stays in the worktree that ran it (its `wayford.sqlite` and `runs/`), which not even the main checkout on the same machine sees. So the ten-seed reports and the sweep are prepared again on the presenting machine before 2026-10-14, after `pnpm seed:demo` (section 3).

## Risks

- **Time.** Per goal: Scout's three walks, 30 runs at five at a time, the Analyst, then the Lens and the Race. About 30 to 45 minutes a goal, an hour and a half with the sweep. `pnpm dev` stays up the whole time, with the user's `.env` in place.
- **The site changes** before the finals. G2 hangs on one job offer, and offers close. Each pass checks it, and the record is dated.
- **A blocking gap bigger than S.** It is listed and goes back to `s1-07`'s list. With the cutoff on Tuesday, the user hears about it the day it is found.
- **A run writes to the site.** The team accepted it for `s1-07`, and the watch kills io on the first typed field. It cannot stop a write that needs no typing, and Scout and the sweep are only checked after the fact.
- **Load.** Five runs at once on one machine slow each step. The budget is simulated time since `s2-09`, so load no longer spends a persona's budget, but the wall time measured here is this machine's.
- **`docs/DEMO.md` moves under us.** `demo-1` edits it this week. This task touches only "Finals: any target", and the branch merges `origin/main` before the PR.
