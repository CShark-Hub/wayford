# s2-08 — B4: misclick on small targets: spec

Task: `tasks/s2-08-misclick.md` · Branch: `feature/s2-08-misclick` · Date: 2026-10-08
Status: approved 2026-10-08

## Goal

A click on a target smaller than the persona's motor limit misses with a probability from the effective-width model, rolled and logged, and lands on the nearest neighbour, so a persona with a motor limit fails small targets with its hand and not only with its eyes.

## Constraints that bind

- B4: "for targets smaller than the persona's motor threshold, with probability from the effective-width model the click lands on the nearest neighbour", sourced to ISO 9241-9 and MacKenzie 1992. "The formula for `p`, from the target's size and the persona's limit, and its source, go into the B4 row before the code" (task).
- "Seeing a target and hitting it are two capabilities." The spec picks one of three options for how P4 and B4 share target size, and says why (task).
- "Vera's hard P4 cut keeps closing the entrances to routes B and C (`s1-04`): that is what leaves the contrast sweep on Vera with one route" (task).
- Dice: "one roll per click on a small target, `p` from the model, logged fired or not. A target at or above the limit rolls nothing"; each `rolled` is "the next draw of the run seed's behaviour stream"; "a rule that does not apply to the action logs nothing"; "without a fired roll the two actions are identical" (task; `docs/RULES.md` stage 7).
- "Where a miss lands is fixed by geometry once the roll fires, never by a second random draw, so a seed reproduces it. The spec defines 'nearest' (from the click point, over shown elements)" (task).
- "The cursor goes to the point the click actually lands (F5 AC7)" (task).
- "Claim less: a B4 miss is a hypothesis from a model with a source, and the report never presents it as a measured miss" (task; `AGENTS.md`, "Traps").
- Core already holds B4's shape: `DICE_RULES` includes `B4`, a B4 roll is legal on a proposed `click` only, a rule rolls at most once per action, and a performed action may differ from the proposed one only beside a fired roll (`packages/core/src/run.ts`, `checkDice`).
- "Provenance on every threshold" (`AGENTS.md`). A px threshold carries its source.
- Determinism (`docs/CONVENTIONS.md`): no `Math.random`, behaviour rolls from one stream of the run seed, logged with the rolled value.
- Out of scope: movement time (s2-09), tremor, overshoot or any other cursor path animation, misses on keys or scrolls (task). Files: `packages/agent/src/**`, `docs/RULES.md`, and `packages/core/src/profile.ts` with the persona fixtures only if the motor limit becomes its own knob (task).

**No fixture sets the limit yet** (decided with the user at this spec, 2026-10-08). The one persona it could go on today without moving P4 is Milo, with 48 above his 44 px cut. Under the model below, that would roll only on 46 px controls with a control in reach: the open menu's links, the checkbox pairs and the `/settings` buttons. Each roll would be at p 0.009, a token. What the option means in words, for RULES: an engineering division, not two measured capabilities. P4 removes a target below the persona's cut from the observation; B4 adds a modelled miss rate on the targets P4 leaves. A persona whose P4 floor (its threshold, for a hard cut) is at or above its motor limit never rolls B4.

### The formula, in `docs/RULES.md` before the code

`W` is the target's `targetPx` (the smaller side of the area a click activates, "What is measured"), `L` the persona's `misclick.threshold`. ISO 9241-9 takes a pointer's endpoints as normal around the aim point, and its effective width as 4.133 standard deviations, the width that holds 96% of them (MacKenzie 1992). Read the persona's limit as that width, `σ = L / 4.133`, and a tap misses a target of width `W` when it lands more than `W / 2` from the aim point along the target's smaller side, which happens with probability `1 − erf(k · W / L)`, `k = 4.133 / (2√2) ≈ 1.46124` (the error model of Wobbrock, Cutrell, Harada and MacKenzie 2008). B4 rolls what the target's smallness adds to the miss rate the same persona has at its limit:

```
p = erf(k) − erf(k · W / L),   k = 4.133 / (2√2),   for W < L
```

`erf(k) = 0.961219`, the hit rate at the limit. `p` is 0 at `W = L` and rises as the target shrinks, so "a target at or above the limit rolls nothing" holds without a jump. Worked examples, computed with an erf outside our code (Python's `math.erf`): `W = L / 2` gives `p = 0.961219 − erf(0.730618) = 0.262706`; 30 px under 44, `0.120061`; 36 under 44, `0.052099`; 46 under 48, `0.008878`; `W = 0`, `0.961219`.

What the formula assumes, said in RULES: reading a limit as a 96% effective width is Wayford's assumption (`folk`), whatever source the number itself has; the error model was measured with a mouse in one dimension, and in it the spread grows with the distance and speed of the movement, both of which B4 ignores by fixing it at `L / 4.133`; the baseline misses a persona makes on a target at its limit are not modelled for any target.

### Where the miss lands

- **The aim point** is where the click would have landed: the target's `actionPoint` (`act.ts`), the centre of the part of its box inside the viewport.
- **Candidates** are the shown controls of the step's observation: the elements with a ref (`state` not `invisible`) and a measured `targetPx`, which only a control carries (`measurement.ts`), the target excluded. A text block is no candidate: a tap that lands on text does nothing, like one on empty space, and a click on a wide paragraph would be pressed at its centre, hundreds of px from where the miss fell. Also excluded: a control whose box holds the aim point (one that encloses the target), and a control whose own action point lies inside the target's box (a click there lands on the target).
- **Distance** is the Euclidean distance from the aim point to the nearest point of a candidate's box: a tap lands on the neighbour once it crosses the neighbour's edge, not its centre.
- **Reach.** A candidate is in reach when its distance is at most `L`. With none in reach, B4 does not apply to the click: it takes no draw and logs nothing. Along one axis the model puts fewer than 1 in 20,000 taps further than `L` from the aim point, in the plane about 1 in 5,000. A miss onto empty space is a click on nothing, which an `Action` cannot carry, so it is left out.
- **Nearest** is the candidate in reach with the smallest distance. A tie goes to the candidate earlier in the observation's element order, the order the Actor reads them (`e1..eN`).
- **The click lands** on the neighbour's action point, as any click on it would: the performed action is `{ type: "click", ref: <neighbour> }`, or a `doubleClick` when B1 fired, and `performAction` moves the cursor there before it presses (F5 AC7). An `Action` names a ref, not a point, so the press is where a click on that neighbour always lands, not where the miss crossed its edge. Nothing about it is random once the roll fired.

On the target app at the defaults, from the measured boxes: with a limit of 48 on desktop, each checkbox on `/deliveries` and on `/settings` has the other in reach (35 px to its box), and the two buttons on `/settings` each other (40 px); of the nav links only "Home", 76 px wide, reaches "Your account" (46 px), since a wider link's neighbour lies further off its centre; on the phone the open menu's links are 8 px apart (31 px), and the menu toggle has no control in reach. With 44 on `/settings/data`, every one of the seven links has the ones above and below it at 28 px, and the tie goes to the one above: a miss on "Fulfilment ledger" lands on "Contact details". The isolated small controls, the kebab (28 px), "Your data" (28), "Order archive" (36) and the 20 px text links, have no control within 48 px on either device, so B4 never rolls on them: on this app B4 is about crowded targets.

**What B4 gets wrong, said in RULES.** It sends every miss it rolls to one neighbour, so `p` is not the model's chance of landing on that neighbour: that chance is lower where a gap lies between them (most misses land just off the target's edge) and can be higher where the neighbour's label abuts the target. The aim point is the centre of the control's own box, which for a checkbox inside its label is not the centre of the area a click activates, and a square target can be missed on both axes: both make real misses likelier than `p`, so B4 understates there. Misses onto text or empty space, and so every miss on a control with no control in reach, are not modelled: B4 understates there too. The press lands on the neighbour's own action point, not where the miss crossed its edge.

**B2 after a miss.** The history keeps the action the Actor proposed (`actor-loop.ts`). After a miss that leaves the page reading the same, such as a toggled checkbox, an Actor that proposes the same click again repeats itself in B2's sense, so B2 may roll on it. RULES says so; it is B2's rule as written, not a new one.

### Agent: `packages/agent/src/misclick.ts` (new, pure)

```ts
/** k in p = erf(k) − erf(k · W / L): ISO 9241-9's 4.133 standard deviations, halved, over √2. */
export const MISS_SCALE = 4.133 / (2 * Math.SQRT2);

/** B4's p for a target of `targetPx` under a motor limit of `limitPx`, `0 ≤ targetPx < limitPx`. */
export function missProbability(targetPx: number, limitPx: number): number;

/** What B4 resolves for a click: its p and the neighbour a miss lands on. */
export interface Misclick { p: number; neighbour: ElementRef }

/**
 * B4 for a click on `ref` in the step's evidence: undefined when the rule does not apply (no shown
 * element with that ref, no measured `targetPx`, a target at or above the limit, no shown control
 * in reach).
 */
export function misclickOf(ref: ElementRef, page: FullObservation, limitPx: number): Misclick | undefined;
```

`erf` is a private function: its Maclaurin series, a fixed 40 terms, `t_0 = x`, `t_n = t_{n−1} × (−x²) / n`, summand `t_n / (2n + 1)`, summed from `n = 39` down to 0, times `2/√π` written as a literal (`1.1283791670955126`). Only `+ − × ÷`, in a fixed order, so the same `p` comes out to the bit on every engine and a stored roll's `fired` replays; its value is within a few ulps of erf over `[0, k]`, which is all `W < L` reaches. A text block clicked has no `targetPx` (P4 reads controls only), so B4 does not apply to it.

### Agent: `packages/agent/src/behaviour.ts`

```ts
export interface BehaveStep {
  /** Whether the proposal repeats the Actor's previous one (B2, `history.ts`). */
  repeated: boolean;
  /** The step's evidence: B4 reads the target's size and the shown elements from it. */
  page: FullObservation;
}

export function behave(
  proposed: Action,
  behaviour: Pick<Behaviour, "doubleClick" | "scrollWhenStuck" | "misclick">,
  draw: () => number,
  step: BehaveStep,
): Behaved;
```

The rules roll in RULES' table order, B1, B2, B4. B4 applies when the proposed action is a `click`, the profile sets `misclick`, B2 did not fire (the click is still performed), and `misclickOf` resolves. Then `rolled = draw()`, `fired = diceFires(p, rolled)`, `{ rule: "B4", p, rolled, fired }` is pushed, and a fired roll moves the performed action's ref to the neighbour, keeping its type: a double click B1 made misses as a double click. `step` is required, so a caller cannot leave B4 off by forgetting the page. A profile without `misclick` takes no extra draw, so every run of today's personas replays its rolls unchanged.

### Agent: `packages/agent/src/actor-loop.ts`

`observe` returns `{ full, shown }` instead of `shown` alone, and `walk` passes `{ repeated, page: full }` to `behave`. Nothing else moves: the full observation is already emitted on `stepObserved`, the backend still gets `shown` alone, the history keeps `shown` and the proposed action, and `performAction` aims at the performed ref in `shown`. A wasted step still draws nothing.

### What the record and the report show

The step keeps `proposedAction` (the Actor's click), `performedAction` (the click on the neighbour) and the B4 roll with its `p`. io's Analyst case writes such a step as "click on X (planned as click on Y, changed by B4)" (`packages/io/src/analyst-digest.ts`), and the Analyst writes `hypothesis` findings only (`packages/io/src/analyst.ts`), while code's own findings come from the route table and axe, which B4 never touches. So the Analyst never presents a B4 miss as a measured finding. Two gaps stay outside this task's files and become follow-ups: io's `provenanceOf` gives B4 no basis entry, so a hypothesis citing it loses its source, and run detail prints a B4 roll like B1's, with no word that it is modelled. No fixture sets the limit, so neither shows in any run until the follow-up gives Milo one.
